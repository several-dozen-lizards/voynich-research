# Are rare g endings partly a reading or handwriting issue?

> Plain-English reading guide to the archived snapshot. The linked reports contain the full rules, results, and qualifications. This guide is editorial material added after the original archive.

## What we tested

Inspected selected manuscript images and compared g/m classifications and positions across transcriptions.

## What we found

Graphic and encoding differences are plausible contributors to the apparent rarity. Some g-classified forms occur inside lines, so a strict line-ending-only account does not hold for all inspected examples.

## What remains unresolved

The audit did not establish a universal g-to-m replacement or an expert decision that the forms are one sign. No names or sound values were refitted.
