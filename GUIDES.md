# Plain-English research guides

Start with the [README overview](README.md) for the broad findings. Each linked guide explains the question, findings, limitations, folder contents, and owning evidence. These are summaries of the archived snapshot, including older and superseded checkpoints.

Repeated trial/seed directories are explained by their parent study. A thousand trial files do not necessarily represent a thousand independent experiments.

## What has worked or shown promise so far

The strongest leads concern **written structure**. The tool successes below are useful foundations for testing ideas; they do not establish a language, sound system, or translation.

### Manuscript patterns worth following

- **Some section differences survive fairly demanding controls.** Several word-part families retained their direction across matched comparisons, retrospective folio splits, and removal of frequent training words. These are useful structural observations to explain, with meanings still unknown. [Word-part study](guides/studies/word-parts-v50.md)
- **The local sh/ch–qok association has survived several specified retrospective tests.** Qualified comparisons found a next-word association in both readings of the same manuscript, including available-follower comparisons. The remaining challenge is distinguishing spelling, junction, phrase, and grammatical explanations; the readings are not independent replications. [Re-adjudication](guides/studies/sh-qok-retrospective-readjudication-v1.md) · [Currier A comparison](guides/studies/currier-a-available-only-v1.md)
- **Certain paragraph-opening words are atypical under a simple spelling model.** The hand-2 botanical comparison passed in both readings and retained its direction on all seven test folios. This is a candidate structural signal, with a small training sample and retrospective split; it does not identify names or sounds. [Template-breaker study](guides/studies/template-breaker-v65.md)

### Tools and methods that have worked within a stated scope

- **The unknown-context scorer repair fixed a real bias.** Ignoring context features absent from training improved several development measures and made unsupported decisions explicit. Abstentions and the less favorable strict-recall measure remain part of the result; broad role recovery is still unqualified. [Scorer repair](guides/studies/contextual-role-unknown-fix-v109.md)
- **Some known-answer pipelines passed their declared controls.** The later v7.2 evaluator qualified on its admitted scope, and the later-month-annotation matcher passed its finite calibration bank. The screened published keys were still not testable at intake, and that matcher did not run an annotation target fit; its separate receipt defect is documented. [Evaluator history](guides/studies/ordered-name-lists-v7c.md) · [Annotation-matcher calibration](guides/studies/voynich-lab--extensions--phase9_month_annotations_v1.md)
- **The inventories and audit trail are working research infrastructure.** Complete written-form inventories, concordances, source ledgers, and file checks make observations and mistakes traceable. Source and image audits have already exposed consequential reading, annotation, and exposure problems. These tools can support the next well-specified question now. [Complete atlas](guides/studies/complete-atlas-v56.md) · [Concordance](guides/studies/contrast-context-v58.md) · [Source-adapter audit](guides/studies/voynich-lab--results--jx--JX-20261107-LSIB-1.md)

## How to read the open questions and next steps

The tables describe the archived checkpoint. **“What would move this forward?” is an editorial planning suggestion**, not a new protocol or a claim that the work has run. Some historical attempts have a completed successor or a terminal stopping rule: their appropriate next step is to use that successor or preserve the stop, rather than rerun the old attempt. Any changed scientific question still needs its own justified design and controls.
