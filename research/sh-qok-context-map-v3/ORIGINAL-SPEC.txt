## 0. Context you won't have

- **The lead.** A paragraph word opening ***sh-*** is directly followed by a word opening ***qok-*** more often than a word opening ***ch-*** is. This holds within the same page and the same ending family (*eey / edy / ey / y / other*, matched longest first).
- **Single-target retest, attempt C (24 Oct).** Panel P1 was scribe 2 outside bath: 20 botanical folios (f26–f55, all searched in v75/v77) plus 6 Rosettes panels (f85r1, f85r2, f86v3–v6). It passed in both readings:
  - H: +5.9 points [+0.6, +11.6], Holm-over-4 p = .041
  - F: +6.4 points [+1.5, +11.3], same p
  - All nulls passed, including a junction-only generator.
  - Panel P2 (scribe 3 outside starred) was not estimable.
- **Discovery panels (descriptive only, never tested inferentially).** Bath, scribe 2: +9.8 H / +8.7 F. Starred, scribe 3: +10.5 H / +9.9 F.
- **What's still open:**
  - (a) **Availability.** A sh/ch gap can come purely from *which followers were able to be qok*. If the next word is itself a sh/ch-opening word, it can't be qok, and sh and ch might differ in how often that happens. ChatGPT's synthetic work showed an aggregate 15-point gap can arise from unequal availability alone. A test conditioned on availability pattern (support rule W ≥ 160) qualified on synthetic layouts at W ≈ 160.3–168.
  - (b) **Context.** Is this one rule with different strength across scribes and sections, or different rules in different places? It has never been asked directly. Currier A (scribe 1) failed in the v75 search, but botanical also failed there and then passed as a single target, so A needs its own single-target test.

## 3. Part A: availability-conditioned test

### A.1 Availability rule (frozen)

> A follower is **available** if it is not itself a sh/ch-family opener: it does not open with `ch`, `sh`, `cth`, `ckh`, `cph` or `cfh`.
> Otherwise it is **blocked**.

- This uses the follower's opening class but not whether it is `qok`, so support stays blind to the outcome.
- The estimand becomes: *among followers that could have been qok-type, is qok more frequent after sh than after ch?*
- **Pre-declared sensitivity A.1b** (reported, not inferential): also count followers opening with `qo` but not `qok` as available. This is the same as A.1, so check it gives identical availability and record that. Then try a stricter variant: available only if the follower opens with `q`, `o`, `d`, `y` or `a`. Report the effect under both.

### A.2 Engine

The availability-pattern exact conditional test qualified at W ≥ 160 (the "Outcome-relevant support" and "Just above W = 160" runs), **unchanged**. Prefix orientations are permuted only within the same exact-body discordant group **and** the same ordered pair of follower-availability flags.

### A.3 Cells and support (outcome-blind)

| Cell | Material | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A-P1** | Attempt C's P1 folio list, unchanged | Primary for Part A (exposed; retrospective) |
| **A-bath** | Scribe 2, Balneological | New question on a discovery panel |
| **A-starred** | Scribe 3, Starred paragraphs | New question on a discovery panel |

For each cell × reading, compute W, the number of contributing pages and the largest page share of W.

**Gate:** W ≥ 160, at least 8 contributing pages, no page over 25% of W.

- If a cell fails the gate, it is NOT ESTIMABLE for that reading.
- No pooling, and no adding folios afterwards.

### A.4 Controls at real support

Use synthetic layouts matched to each cell's actual stratum and availability structure, with these scenarios:

- null low and high;
- additive page/line/position null;
- junction-only null;
- **availability-only null**: sh and ch differ only in their rate of blocked followers, with equal qok rates among available followers;
- positive: .20 vs .35 among available followers.

**Gates:** null upper Wilson-95 bound ≤ .05; positive lower bound ≥ .80. If the positive gate fails, the cell is UNDERPOWERED: run it, label it, and don't change the design.

### A.5 Inference

- One-sided, sh > ch among available followers.
- **Holm over 3 per reading** (A-P1, A-bath, A-starred).
- The Part A headline is a pass in **both** H and F for a cell.
- **Interpretation:** if A-P1 or a discovery cell passes, the sh→qok gap is not explained by follower availability. If the conditioned effect is near zero while attempt C's aggregate effect stands, availability is the leading explanation.
- Report the **share of the aggregate gap that remains after conditioning**, descriptively, with a folio-bootstrap CI.

### B.2 Per-context estimates (all descriptive)

### B.3 Inferential questions (Holm over 2 per reading)

**B-Q1: Currier A.** Does sh > ch → qok hold in scribe 1?

- One cell: **C6 + C7 together**, the only pre-declared pooling in this spec. Same scribe and variety; strata stay folio × ending.
- Same exact engine and support gate as attempt C (V ≥ 80, at least 8 pages, no page over 25%).
- Same controls at real support.
- One-sided, sh > ch.
- If qok is too rare in A to give support, report NOT ESTIMABLE. That is itself informative about A.

**B-Q2: Heterogeneity in Currier B.** Across C1–C5, is the effect's strength different between contexts?

- Fit a random-effects model on the per-context MH estimates (REML), giving between-context variance τ² with a Q-profile 95% CI, plus I².
- **Test:** a permutation test of heterogeneity. Reassign whole folios between contexts **within scribe** (scribe 2's folios among C1–C3, scribe 3's among C4–C5), keeping each folio's events intact, and recompute Q. The p-value is the share of shuffles with Q at or above the observed value.
- This asks whether the section matters within a scribe, which is the question Re actually asked. Use at least 10,000 shuffles, or exact enumeration if feasible.
- **Known bias:** C1 and C4 were selected because the effect was large there (winner's curse). That inflates apparent heterogeneity. Pre-declared sensitivity: repeat B-Q2 on scribe 2 only, with and without C1. Report both and label the selection.

**B-Q2 controls:**

- a homogeneous positive (same +8 points in every context; must not flag, upper bound ≤ .05);
- a heterogeneous positive (+12 points in one context and 0 in the rest, at the real support; report power descriptively, with no gate);
- a homogeneous null.

### B.4 Reading the map

- **Currier A passes:** the rule spans both "languages". It's a scribe-independent writing habit or structure.
- **A not estimable or no pass:** the rule is unconfirmed outside Currier B, and possibly B-specific.
- **B-Q2 flags heterogeneity within a scribe:** the section changes the rule's strength. That's context-dependence in the sense Re raised: the same scribe writes differently by content.
- **B-Q2 shows no heterogeneity, with a tight τ:** one rule at roughly constant strength per scribe, where bath's larger estimate may be the winner's curse.
- Wide τ intervals mean "can't tell". Say that, not "no heterogeneity".

## 6. What this can and cannot say

- **Availability:** it can show the gap survives (or doesn't survive) conditioning on whether a qok-type follower was possible. It cannot separate junction spelling from grammar among available followers.
- **Currier A:** it can show whether the habit crosses into A. A no-pass at low support cannot exclude a weaker effect.
- **Heterogeneity:** it can show whether section changes strength within a scribe. It cannot say *why*. Image-defined context (a blind rater coding what is pictured beside each line) is the separate, prospective follow-up and is **not** part of this spec.
- **Nothing here** is non-circular confirmation, a meaning, or a mechanism claim beyond what each test directly measures.
