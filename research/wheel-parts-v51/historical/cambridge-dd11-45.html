Cambridge, University Library, MS Dd.11.45

Medical treatises

Contents

Introduction: This composite manuscript of three parts contains medical treatises and recipes in Latin and Middle English, including excerpts from the Liber de Sinthomatibus Mulierum that was part of an ensemble of texts that was known under the title Trotula. The manuscript also contains magical texts. The most significant text among these is the Liber de angelis annulis karectibus et ymaginibus planetum (Book of Angels, Rings, Characters, and Images of the Planets), which provides detailed instructions for conjuring angels. The manuscript also features a number of added instructions with angelic characters, charms, and an elaborate necromantic instruction for a ritual aimed at raising the soul of a deceased person. The necromantic instruction has been transcribed by M.R. James , who used it as the basis for his publication The Experiment: A New Year's Ghost Story in 1931. Although James's transcription is accurate overall, he has misidentified the name of one of the spirits on whose permission the ritual's success relies. The spirit whose name James transcribes as 'Raffaell', presumably the Archangel Raphael, in fact is called 'Assaell', referring to the fallen angel Azazel. With this correction it has been possible to identify the necromantic instruction as a copy of a work that circulated widely in magical manuscripts in late medieval and early modern England. Dr Clarck Drieshen| Project Cataloguer| Curious Cures in Cambridge Libraries|

History

Provenance and Acquisition

Sir Thomas Knyvett (1539-1618) : Knyvett's shelfmark is inscribed in the lower margin of f. 153v: 'Sed ― 53'. See McKitterick (1978), p. 163 (MS 53).

Thomas Knyvett (1596-1658) : grandson of Sir Thomas Knyvett 1539-1618, inherited this manuscript together with Knyvett's collection. See McKitterick (1978), pp. 33-34..

John Knyvett (1623-1673) : great-grandson of Sir Thomas Knyvett 1539-1618 and son of Thomas Knyvett (1596-1658), inherited this manuscript together with Knyvett's collection. See McKitterick (1978), p. 36 .

Thomas Knyvett (d. 1693) : great-great-grandson of Sir Thomas Knyvett 1539-1618 and son of John Knyvett (1623-1673), inherited this manuscript together with Knyvett's collection. Thomas died without heir and the Knyvett library was fragmented after his death: 57 of Knyvett's manuscripts had been acquired by John Moore [see below] by 1698. See McKitterick (1978), p. 37.

John Moore (1646-1714) , bishop of Ely: no. 195 (inscribed on f. 1r) in Bernard, Catalogi librorum manuscriptorum Angliæ et Hiberniæ (c. 1697).

Presented to the University Library in 1715 by George I : donation bookplate engraved by John Pine in 1736 on the front pastedown.

16. 126v-128v
Collection of medical recipes
Language(s): Latin and Middle English

Contains Middle English recipes for healing the eyes and strangury ('stranguriam', corr. for 'sturuguriam' in Connolly (2009), p. 67 [no. 6])

Two Latin recipes for curing a headache and blurry vision caused by reuma on f. 128r are attributed to a physician named Albon (‘Quod D. Albone’), perhaps Edmund Albon, 'doctor of physick', who was rector of the church of St John the Baptist, Garboldisham, Norfolk, in 1478 (see Francis Blomefield, 'Hundred of Giltcross: Snarehill-House', in An Essay Towards A Topographical History of the County of Norfolk, Volume 1: Containing the Hundreds of Diss, Giltcross, Shropham, the Burgh of Thetford, Grimeshoe, Wayland, and Forehoe (London: Miller, 1805), p. 271.

The recipes are as follows:

  • f. 126v: for migraine, an aposteme in the head, for dropsy and all manner of aches in the head; to make a precious water to clean sore and blind eyes; two against strangury (slow or painful emission of urine)
  • f. 127r: two for deafness, blindness and other ailments of the head; two for failing eyesight; for strangury and kidney problems, colic and the stone; two for all ailments of the head
  • f. 127v: for terror or nausea and lack of appetite; for blood in one's urine and pain in the back or abdomen; five further recipes for unstated purposes, but perhaps the same as the last item
  • f. 128r: two for head pain and blindnessl a mundificative for the kidneys and bladder; a repercussive to put in one's eyes to remove matter; a recipe for 'dragetum'
  • f. 128v: a conserve to purify the blood
