Which section differences remain?

23 October 2026 · v49 · Prefixes, suffixes and strictly internal EVA sequences

16 overlapping feature/position contrasts passed the two-reading screen. They belong to a few related written families, not sixteen independent discoveries. No meanings, syllables or morphemes are identified.

The clearest surviving contrasts

  • Hand 2, Currier B, paragraph interiors: qo- occurs in 25.64% of bath words versus 10.40% of botanical words in H; 25.65% versus 10.83% in F. These are equal-folio mean percentages. The increase also appears at line starts.
  • Hand 1, Currier A, paragraph interiors: strictly internal ke occurs in 16.04% of Recipes words versus 2.26% of botanical words in H; 15.17% versus 2.25% in F. Related keo and oke sequences overlap this same family.
  • Same hand-1 comparison: -eol is 11.19% versus 1.99% in H and 10.61% versus 1.93% in F, higher in Recipes.
  • Hand 2 interiors: internal od and terminal -ody are more frequent in botanical text than baths. They are overlapping spelling observations, not independent evidence for an od semantic core.

What was held fixed

Exact Davis hand, Currier variety, source text format and line-position category; only loci present in both H and F. H and F were counted separately. Numbered folios combine recto/verso and foldout surfaces, and receive equal weight. The estimable comparisons were hand1/A/P botanical versus Recipes (47 versus6 folios), and hand2/B/P baths versus botanical (10 versus10 folios), each split into first/interior/last word.

Interior is initially a broad category. Every passing interior feature was additionally checked separately at word positions2,3,4, and every passing feature was checked among words5–8 EVA characters long. All estimable directions persisted. Several effects attenuated substantially: interior -ol and qol- fell below2 percentage points in the length-restricted subset. These sensitivities were not independently significance-tested.

945 features were tested in12 transcription/panel combinations. The screen uses1999 folio-label permutations, max-statistic correction across features, then Bonferroni across panels; it additionally requires the same direction and at least2 percentage points in both readings. The small permutation p-values depend on exchangeability assumptions; quires, physical layout, word families and residual topic groupings remain uncontrolled. This is exploratory conditional association, not a causal subject effect or fresh external replication.

Interpretation

The surviving patterns show section-linked written conventions within a hand, beyond the broad line-position mix. They may reflect vocabulary, constructions, spelling choices or local conventions. H/F agreement establishes robustness to these readings of the same manuscript, not independent replication. No ending was merged and no role classifier was applied.
