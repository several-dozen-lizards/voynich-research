§457–458 table transcription and source readback

Witness: R. Ramsay Wright, The Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology (London: Luzac, 1934), English extract hosted by Skyscript. The title dates al-Biruni's work to 1029. PDF pages 63–64, printed pages 62–63. This is one translated witness, not verification against the Arabic manuscript or a second edition.

Both table images were read twice by the same agent; this is a readback, not independent human verification. TABLES.json retains each printed run, then expands it to an operational 30-degree sequence. All 24 row totals equal 30. Totals are an error check, not grounds to change a faint digit. The enlarged source actually shows Aries d8.

§457: the source explicitly says the starred entries mark female degrees. We normalize those entries to F and the unstarred entries to M. The surrounding discussion describes disagreements and alternative ordering schemes. Those alternatives are not added to this experiment. The table is one historical scheme, not a universal degree classification. Its category names are not evidence for the sex of any Voynich figure.

§458: normalized codes b=brilliant, L=luminous, d=dusky, s=dark or shadowed, v=empty or void. The source's dark-category type looks s-like with a diacritic; s is an explicit normalization, not a new category. L occasionally resembles I. The author warns that books differ on these classifications. This test addresses only the transcribed table.

Preserved oddities: §457 Libra ends F7 F2; §458 Taurus ends b3 b2. These are not silently corrected or collapsed in the run transcription. Expansion yields adjacent identical categories. Aries §458 third entry is d8, not the initial low-resolution impression d6; both source images remain available.

Degree expansion: interpret printed counts as consecutive run lengths starting at degree 1. A common phase search accounts for unknown image starting point; no per-sign phase fitting is admitted. This operational interpretation should be checked against another textual witness before a historical identification is claimed.

Scope: this artifact transcribes the two numeric tables and records the operative prose/legend in paraphrase; it is not a diplomatic transcription of all Arabic headings or surrounding prose.
