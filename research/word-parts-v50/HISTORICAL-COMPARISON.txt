Historical comparison design and source audit — 2026-10-23

Status: source selection and prospective design, not a historical result.
Do not call Voynich prefixes or suffixes a recovered grammar. Compare position-sensitive statistical profiles first; any mapping of grammatical roles must be learned from known texts and validated on another manuscript before application to Voynich.

Primary source lead: Cambridge Curious Cures, https://collectionsresearch.lib.cam.ac.uk/curious-cures
Project describes digitisation/cataloguing/conservation of over 180 medieval manuscripts containing medical recipes. This is a source pool, not a ready validated line- and hand-annotated dataset.
Concrete catalogue inspected: https://medieval.lib.cam.ac.uk/catalogue/Trinity/MS-TRINITY-COLLEGE-O-00002-00013
This is composite. Part 1 includes a text dated 1542 and later annotations; catalogue contains selected incipits/explicits as well as some full short transcriptions. Do not tokenize the catalogue as manuscript prose or treat the entire codex as fifteenth century. A manuscript-unit/date and transcription-coverage audit is required before inclusion.
Existing Casebooks materials are useful later-period controls, not period-matched confirmation. Existing expanded Latin source texts cannot establish distributions of unexpanded manuscript shorthand.

Prospective rules before scoring any new historical source:
1. Preserve diplomatic and expanded text separately where supplied, align them; never silently expand shorthand. Retain physical lines, folio, hand, language and genre supplied by source. Missing metadata stays missing.
2. Separate prose, instructional passages, tabular/field entries and annotations using editor-supplied roles or independently verified labels. The earlier missing-label problem remains unresolved; do not invent independent validation.
3. Apply the same exhaustive contiguous-feature extraction, folio incidence, concentration and top-five removal checks. Normalize frequency per word; preserve length and alphabet-size sensitivity; compare rank/position distributions, not literal qo/eol spelling across languages.
4. Compare compositional freedom (distinct flanking contexts and conditional entropy), adjacency of starts/ends with line edges fixed, within-folio repetition, first/interior/last profiles. Keep actual word frequencies and line lengths in null controls. Minimum support and multiplicity rules must be frozen for each declared comparison family before outcome inspection.
5. Hold out an entire manuscript for transfer, with folio-grouped development splits. Compare instructional/annotation patterns with same-hand ordinary prose when available. Include shuffled role labels, frequency-matched random word sets and layout-only controls. Fit no Voynich-targeted code or vocabulary mapping.
6. A distribution match is compatibility only. Applying a proposed encoding/grammar transformation requires explicit reversible rules, known-text recovery tests, wrong-rule controls and retained failures. No current result licenses semantic identification.

Unresolved prerequisite: adequate period-matched diplomatic text with reliable hand/line metadata and contextual-role labels. No distributional result is claimed for this source lead.
