Data quantity versus legal rearrangements

Frozen factorial diagnosis: 1,800 fresh synthetic datasets, two methods on each, all 3,600 results retained. No manuscript files read; no H/F pooling. Three independent axes: 6/24/96 lines per page; fragmented versus aligned body locations; original fixed position pairs versus body-only selected position pairs.

The selected method examines all 105 disjoint pairings of eight positions and chooses using body support only, before examining prefix or qok values. Both methods preserve line composition, exact-position margins and literal body matching. Selection changes the disjoint subspace; it does not necessarily enlarge each individual null, and does not enumerate the complete fiber of all legal arrangements.

Effects are either zero with additive page/line/position nuisance, a .15 local increase, or a .45 increase. Each cell has 100 datasets. A flag uses exact conditional p with Bonferroni8. “Attainable” counts datasets whose best possible score could pass .05 under their own fixed margins/outcomes. A nonconstant test may still have no attainable significant score.

How to interpret the comparisons

Changing method on identical data tests how much the fixed pairing discards. Increasing independent lines tests support volume, and aligning body locations at equal volume tests comparability. Neither more observations nor more legal states guarantees a large enough outcome-sensitive score range. Compare actual detection with attainable significance, not just null-space size.

Any sparse failure is retained. A lack of improvement from this selected pairing does not prove that every possible rearrangement is exhausted. The matching score ignores prefix/outcomes and therefore need not maximize actual informative support. These are event-level synthetic arrays, not a corpus parser, a grammatical interpretation or evidence that any Voynich association is absent.
