Positional-first shared-word fallback v112 — 25 October 2026

ANSWER
The fixed fallback preserves additive sharing's decision coverage, but not most of its recovery gain, and it does not demonstrate the requested no-worse confidence quality relative to the repaired baseline. It is a partial tradeoff, not a full solution. No threshold, mixture, calibration or feature rule was changed after viewing these outcomes.

WHAT WAS TESTED
For each original context-word position, use i:surface when that feature is present in the pooled TRAINING vocabulary. If absent globally and the surface is a real word with supported W:surface identity, replace that slot's feature with W:surface. Otherwise leave its unsupported positional feature neutral. A feature missing in only one role is still globally supported and cannot trigger fallback. Test gold labels never determine which identity is activated.
Each occurrence uses four component slots plus the original two pair features. A component occurrence never contributes BOTH its positional and pooled identity. Repeated words in different slots remain distinct occurrences. Boundary markers never receive shared fallback. Original pair-order features, including singletons, remain unchanged. The slot ledger preserves original offsets and the choice of evidence source. For a missing positional feature, the shared contribution pools its identity; its original offset remains metadata, not invented positional training evidence.

FIXED MODELS AND ATTRIBUTION CONTROL
The exact v109 neutral-unknown score function, priors, smoothing formula and1e-12 abstention tolerance are imported unchanged. Actual baseline scores reproduce v109, additive scores reproduce v111. Fallback and local-only controls use the exact same ten-event shared-model counts, vocabulary and denominators as additive; no selective model refit was performed. Baseline uses the original six-event parameterization.
The local-only control activates only the original six features under that ten-event shared model. Its primary-panel recovery and decisions match baseline; mean log loss is.512491, very close to baseline.512097. Fallback is.014036 worse in log loss than this local-only control. Thus changing the shared-model normalizers alone does not account for the result; the fallback contributions themselves materially change outcomes. Both comparator and primary-score differences are retained.

Availability of a positional feature is not evidence that its local estimate is always sufficient; this test used availability only and did not fit a reliability threshold.

DECISION
Retain v109 as the reference baseline. Preserve v111 as the observed higher-recovery/higher-coverage candidate with worse probability scores, and v112 as a lower-confidence-cost tradeoff that fails to retain most of v111's recovery gain. Do not adopt v112 as a full resolution of the objective. No fitting or threshold search was continued after this result.
The question left open is how to combine local and shared evidence according to reliability without double-counting or relying solely on global presence. That needs a separately defined development design and an independently selected, sealed confirmation source before selecting another representation. These results do not qualify a general interpolation or calibration scheme. Other branches' sealed reserves remain untouched.
