g / m endings · manuscript image audit

A graphic or transcriptional explanation is plausible. A universal g=m replacement, or an exclusively line-end flourish, is not established.

Eight exposed candidate locations inspected in Yale full scans and native-resolution context crops. Alternate readings remain source records. This is a reader audit, not expert palaeographic adjudication or a sound/name test.

Contact-sheet images are scaled to fit. Open each unaltered crop and context below for detail. The Aries label is rotated for reading; its original context is retained.

What this changes

The anchor has a clear looped/descending final mark; it is not an obvious damaged or missing sign. Several marks rendered g in H are m in F. These observations make the earlier rarity score conditional on a disputed graphic classification. Similar-looking forms across different hands do not establish one grapheme, and agreement on the Aries spelling is retained.

EVA tables distinguish m and g as separate graphic classes; they do not establish different sounds. The historical F transcription was produced using an earlier alphabet and later converted. The 36/38 comparison can reflect classification/encoding choices as well as reader judgment, and is not38 independent expert adjudications.

Position and transcription diagnostics

Across all formats, H has79 word-final g tokens:54 final source tokens,14 interior,8 single-word units and3 first tokens. Circular/radial source endpoints are not ordinary page margins.

Among the79 cases, F is missing at20 loci;16 have differing slot counts and5 differing prefixes/unknowns. The remaining38 have matching slots and exactly the same word prefix: F ends36 with m, one with d and one with y. This strict subset avoids silently aligning changed word boundaries; all excluded cases are retained.

This follow-up is descriptive and was specified after seeing the initial position counts. g/m endings both favour the last source slot compared with other endings, but word family, hand, section and position are not jointly controlled. H f83r.P.5 and agreed hand4 ypcheg f67v1.Y.3 occur before following words; therefore an exclusively physical-line-end explanation is insufficient.

Interpretation retained

  • A distinct graphic sign is possible; the anchor is not unreadable.
  • An allographic, connected-sign or closing-form convention is also possible, supported as a lead by image similarity and systematic g/m transliteration differences.
  • A positional convention may contribute, but is not an exceptionless line-end rule.
  • No phonetic value, semantic function or proper-name status follows from these observations.

No global merge or new anomaly rescore was performed. A separate frozen g/m sensitivity could measure how much of the earlier rank depends on this distinction, while preserving the original readings.

Receipts and limits
