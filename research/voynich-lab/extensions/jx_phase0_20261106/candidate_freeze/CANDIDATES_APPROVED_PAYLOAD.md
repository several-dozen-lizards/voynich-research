## What would be frozen

Approval can freeze the36-row search list, symbolic working priors, scope limits and the rule against selecting candidates from JX results. This does **not** certify every proposed spelling tradition as historically attested or every proposed prior as detectable by JX. Operational row predictions are **NP** until a source-bound candidate reference, spelling/encoding model and detection calibration are fixed before target results. NP is a valid frozen outcome: that row cannot be ranked from the sign of JX after the fact.

All candidates remain hypotheses. There is no accepted language identification in this register. The list is deliberately finite and not exhaustive; group placement is inherited review priority, not a probability ranking or direct evidence that each language occurs in the manuscript.

## Historical window and evidence labels

- Retain1380–1460 as a **working search window**, not a proven writing date or date of composition. The original reports parchment radiocarbon1404–1438; a parchment date is not by itself an ink/text date and does not date an earlier source being copied. The primary measurement receipt was not retrieved in this review; see SOURCE_LEDGER.md for the failed retrieval and the differing public repository dating header. Do not use the narrow range as an absolute exclusion proof.
- Known-script month annotations and marginalia motivate checking traditions; their language, date, author and relation to the main text are separate questions. They are not a crib or direct evidence for each regional language listed. No new image annotation or geographical inference was performed here.
- Rosettes battlement geography is **unverified contextual motivation**, not a criterion that narrows a candidate to northern Italy or the Alps. Later provenance is not origin or language evidence. Exact earliest-owner/date claims remain a source question rather than a selection fact.
- UCC's edited corpus project supports the existence of a substantial Irish vernacular medical tradition [S3]. It does not establish that this codex is Irish or that every desired spelling mechanism was written in1380–1460.
- Primary linguistic scholarship documents Middle Welsh mutation and variable orthographic representation [S4,S5]. It also shows why a written-word/unit proxy need not reliably recover grammatical triggers. This replaces the original blanket claim about standard spelling, not a new language identification.

## Three hypothesis layers and admission status

Each row proposes **L** (language/variety), **S** (specific written representation) and **C** (source script tradition). The unknown Voynich encoding is another layer; a source-script column is not a glyph key. “Phonetic, X ear” means a hypothetical regional spelling model, not a verified tradition. Mixed-language and broad dialect rows are hypothesis families until exact options are enumerated.

To admit a row to an operational comparison, record a named dated witness/edition and exact spans; evidence linking L/S/C; composition vs witness dates; normalized/diplomatic text and uncertainty policy; and any hypothetical phonetic transformation with all alternatives. A missing route is **UNVERIFIED / NOT_TESTED**, not proof that the language is impossible. Older, modern or edited texts can be exploratory surrogates if labeled, but cannot silently stand for a period-specific spelling.

## Prediction vocabulary

- **PROG:** proposed changing-side tendency toward the next word's initial units.
- **REG:** proposed tendency toward the preceding word's final units.
- **WEAK:** a proposed prior of weak visible cross-word conditioning under the specified spelling. It is not a guarantee of a null statistic.
- **NP:** no operational directional forecast. Mixed or uncalibrated representations cannot be assigned one from target outcomes.

**Corrected caveat:** conventional orthographies can represent some boundary-related alternations, and phonetic spelling can also fail to expose the particular unit conditioning JX measures. There is no universal “standard orthography implies WEAK” rule. Mutation can be lexical or syntactic rather than a function of the immediately adjacent glyph; absence of an immediate-unit JX effect cannot rule it out. Cipher/transcription layers and sparse source support can obscure or create statistical associations. No JX class is itself a phonological diagnosis.

The table's **proposed prior** preserves the author's original suggestion as a hypothesis. Its **operational JX prediction** is NP unless a relevant calibrated reference has been frozen. This avoids laundering an unverified historical claim into a known-answer control, while preserving the original ideas for review. No priors were changed in response to JX data; none exist.

## Permitted use, in order

1. **Structural JX:** test position specificity, reading/spacing robustness and unit associations. The candidate list is not used to choose features, favorable strata or stop rules. Readouts are unit-conditioning/reference-profile labels, not language diagnoses.
2. **Candidate comparison:** only source-qualified operational predictions fixed before outcomes may enter a registered compatibility comparison. A symbolic PROG/REG prior is not the numerical prediction of a glyph-unit statistic. NP rows receive no typology ranking. A mismatch reduces support for that tested representation at calibrated sensitivity; it does not eliminate a language.
3. **Fingerprint follow-up:** register finite source/spelling/encoding/transformation families before fitting. A failed mapping rejects that tested configuration at its demonstrated power, not every possible spelling or encoding of the language. Do not claim failure “under any mapping” after a finite search.
4. **Reading attempts:** separately authorize/register a key/transformation search, folio-family heldout split and leakage/exposure ledger, blinded assessment, known-language positive controls and wrong-language/wrong-key/shuffled controls. A procedure that fails its known-answer positive controls cannot validate a reading merely because a wrong-language run also fails. Coherent-looking words or unblinded semantic ratings are insufficient on their own.
