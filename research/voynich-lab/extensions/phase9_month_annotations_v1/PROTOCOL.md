## Frozen source frame

PERIOD_NAMES.json contains one independently cited form for each of ten months in Latin, Italian, German and Occitan: forty language/month cells, no gaps. The candidate lexicon is bounded, not exhaustive. No name will be added after scoring. Exact source forms and dates are retained separately from normalized strings.

Latin: the calendar transcript in the catalogue of the Brandeis Hours, c1420–1430, corroborated by The National Archives' historical Latin reference table. These are scholarly readings/reference lemmas, not newly adjudicated facsimiles. Italian: the Book XII month headings in the 1495 Venetian Piero Crescentio de agricultura; each selected heading was read directly in the saved scan. Magio, Giugnio, Septembre, Octobre, Nouembre and Decembre remain as printed, rather than modernized. The 1495 witness is later than the vellum date; no exact date of the added annotations is inferred. German: explicitly identified historical regional forms/quoted attestations in Grotefend and the Trier Middle High German lexical index; witness dates and undated historical-lemma status are distinguished. Wintermonat is legitimately ambiguous and retained for both November and December. This is a regional historical comparator list, not a claim that all forms coexist in one dialect or manuscript. Occitan: Raynouard's historical entries with medieval calendar/troubadour quotations; edited lemmas are not diplomatic fifteenth-century local witnesses. December Desembre was checked in the printed dictionary scan because its OCR is corrupt. Other admitted lemma forms are legible in the saved entries/quotations.

Annotation source readings are fixed from Zandbergen's MON table. Their Romance language remains disputed. French is not a tested candidate language; a winning language among the four requested languages cannot identify the annotator's language. All twelve panels remain in the denominator, including duplicate April and May panels; these are not independent month identities.

## Normalization and label alternatives

Names in ALL families use the same author-derived Naibbe plaintext normalization: Unicode NFD, remove combining marks, published ligature/special-letter replacements, lowercase letters, w→uu, j→i, k→c. Long s is transcribed/normalized s. No u/v merger, stemming, anagramming, pronunciation inference, spelling correction or manual trimming. Every normalized candidate is checked to use the resulting 23-letter alphabet. Exact diplomatic/edited source strings remain visible.

Cipher/annotation strings use Unicode/letter cleaning without the plaintext w/j/k replacements, preserving encoded symbols, including Naibbe k. This role distinction applies identically to synthetic and known-script target labels; applying plaintext spelling changes to cipher symbols would alter the codebook. Spaces and punctuation are removed uniformly. Parenthesized literal letters are optional groups: enumerate every include/omit combination, without supplying other letters. Thus source notation such as septe(m)b(r) is a finite alternative bundle, not a restoration to a chosen modern spelling. Every alternative bundle travels intact under shuffles. No reversal, arbitrary gap, rotation, edit-distance or partial-word search.

## Families, objective and exact search

One GLOBAL consistent encoding model is shared across all twelve labels in each fit. One candidate language applies globally; no language switching by item. A fit counts the largest number of items whose complete label alternative exactly equals an encoding of the fixed name for that item's month. Denominator twelve; no coverage multiplier or adaptive exclusion. Statistic T is the maximum count (or equivalently count/12) over every declared family, language and submodel.

1. Monoalphabetic: a single injective plaintext-letter→cipher-letter map. Unseen letters may be completed to an injection but are not claimed recovered.
2. Positional variant: three global injective maps, for beginning B, end E, and interior M. First-character precedence makes a singleton B; two-character names use B/E. Position is defined on the entire normalized name, not on newly fitted syllables or label-specific classes. Cipher symbols may recur across different classes.
3. Naibbe verbose: all six published fixed tables alpha, beta1, beta2, beta3, gamma1, gamma2. One table is shared across all labels. A name may be segmented into one/two-letter source units; unigrams use the table's unigram glyph string and bigrams its prefix+suffix strings. All allowed segmentations are searched exactly by dynamic programming, without edit operations. Keep collisions; ambiguity rejection is off, since redrawing the same fixed table cannot remove a deterministic collision. No outer learned glyph substitution. This is a declared fixed-table Naibbe subfamily, not a test of all stochastic mixed-deck variants. Tables were designed using Voynich statistics and are NOT blind controls.

Mono/position fits enumerate compatible item/label-variant constraints with exact finite branch-and-bound. Every constraint enforces forward and reverse consistency in its class. The only pruning rule is an integer upper bound on remaining item count. No optimizer time cutoff, approximate best-found score, random restarts, fitted feature weights or post-outcome budget change. Naibbe enumerates every fixed table and searches all finite unit segmentations. All scores have equal certified upper/lower bounds within these declared models. Exact ties follow family order mono, naibbe, position; language Latin, Italian, German, Occitan; then published table order. This deterministic tie rule is not evidence that a family/language was identified.

## Within-section label-shuffle null and maximum

There is one section in this track: zodiac. Shuffle whole label bundles over all twelve illustration/month slots, preserving their readings/alternatives, duplicate-label multiplicity and bundle structure. Each of 99 fixed permutations refits/reoptimizes EVERY family/language/submodel, and its null score is that full maximum. Observed and shuffled data receive identical search freedom. Upper-tail p=(1+#null maximum ≥ observed maximum)/100. Do not use a single-family or fixed-mapping null after selecting a winning model. Invariant nulls are recorded and cannot provide positive significance.

This statistic can detect length/repetition-pattern coupling as well as learned letter compatibility. It is not uniquely a phonetic-mechanism test. The exact-match lexicon imposes a ceiling under spelling, inflection and dialect mismatch; a negative target result does not rule out arbitrary historical naming conventions or cipher families.

## Calibration bank and gates

Freeze names, source files, code, settings, seeds, ITEM metadata and all shuffle assignments BEFORE generating the synthetic bank. Seeds [9011,9013,9019,9029,9041]. Four languages × eight generating settings (mono, position, six fixed Naibbe keys) × five seeds × positive/negative = 320 datasets. The synthetic manuscript has exactly the same twelve month slots and duplicate April/May identities. German's shared November/December form remains shared. This tests a small fixed crib set, not independent language corpora or new-name generalization.

Mono/position use deterministic seeded permutations of the 23-letter alphabet, globally per family/class. Naibbe generation uses author respacing17/36, one fixed published key, preserving output collisions. Parenthesis ambiguity follows the source support: two optional internal characters for September, one for October/November, zero elsewhere, at deterministic interior indices. The true complete ciphertext is always included. No additional IID noise is introduced in this exact-match qualification. Source truth is saved separately and never supplied to the fitter.

Negative controls use the corresponding positive ciphertext with an independent frozen random permutation over the twelve slots. The same full maximization and 99 null refits apply. There is no imposed conjugacy, quire structure, physical test or hidden key-switching schedule. Null assignments are common across paired cases for each seed; pairs and repeated schedules are not independent replications.

Mapping recovery is assessed in the known generating family/language's fit, whose solver does not see truth. Mono/position must recover EVERY mapping entry actually observed in the twelve labels and match all twelve names; unobserved alphabet entries are excluded. Naibbe must uniquely select the true table among the table-score maxima in that generating-language family and match all twelve names. This recovers the generating codebook identity, not an unobserved stochastic deck sequence or uniquely determined segmentation. The maximum test across all families/languages must also reject the shuffled null. Family identification is not required because position includes mono as a special case. All competing models and ties are reported.

For EACH of 32 language×generating-setting cells, require ≥4/5 positives that jointly recover the true identifiable mapping AND have maximum-test p≤.05, and ≤1/5 negative-control rejections. All ten fits in the cell must complete. All 32 cells must pass; no pooled override. Preserve source/data failures, scorer exceptions, exact-search failures, recovery failures and significance failures separately. Finish the fixed supported bank for diagnostics on failure; keep annotation fits CLOSED and stop with no rescue, name additions, key changes or new registration. Any true-table tie counts as non-unique recovery and fails that trial.
